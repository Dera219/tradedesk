# Market Hours

## Regular trading hours

The main US stock exchanges — NYSE and Nasdaq — are open for regular trading from **9:30 a.m. to
4:00 p.m. Eastern Time**, Monday through Friday. This is when volume is highest and spreads are
tightest.

## Market holidays

The exchanges close on a set of holidays each year, such as New Year's Day, Thanksgiving, and
Christmas. On a few days before a holiday the market closes early, at 1:00 p.m. Eastern. An order
placed on a holiday queues for the next open session.

## Pre-market and after-hours sessions

Extended-hours trading runs roughly 4:00–9:30 a.m. (pre-market) and 4:00–8:00 p.m. (after-hours)
Eastern. It exists, but it carries real disadvantages that matter more than the extra access:

- **Thin volume** — fewer participants, so orders can be hard to fill.
- **Wide spreads** — the gap between bid and ask is larger, so every trade costs more.
- **Sharp moves** — news released after the close can swing prices violently on little volume.

Many brokers only accept limit orders during extended hours, precisely because a market order in a
thin session can fill at a shocking price.

## Why the close and open are special

The open and close concentrate the most volume of the day. Prices can be unusually volatile in the
first and last few minutes as large orders execute, which is worth knowing before placing a market
order right at the bell.
