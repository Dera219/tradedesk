# Dividends

## What a dividend is

A dividend is a payment a company makes to its shareholders, usually in cash and usually quarterly,
distributing part of its profits. Not all companies pay dividends — many growth companies reinvest
everything instead.

## The four dates that matter

- **Declaration date** — the company announces the dividend and its amount.
- **Ex-dividend date** — the cutoff. You must own the shares *before* this date to receive the
  dividend. Buy on or after the ex-date and the seller, not you, gets this payment.
- **Record date** — the company checks its books for who owns shares.
- **Payment date** — the cash actually arrives.

The ex-dividend date is the one that governs whether you receive a given dividend.

## Why the price drops on the ex-date

On the ex-dividend date, the stock's price typically opens lower by roughly the dividend amount.
This is not a loss — the company paid out cash, so each share is worth that much less, and if you
held through the ex-date you receive the dividend that makes up the difference. Buying a stock
"just before the dividend" to capture the payment does not produce free money for this reason.

## Yield

Dividend yield is the annual dividend divided by the share price. A very high yield is not
automatically attractive — it often means the price has fallen sharply on trouble, and a dividend
that looks large today may be cut tomorrow.
