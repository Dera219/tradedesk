# Fees and Commissions

## Commissions

A commission is a fee the broker charges to execute a trade. Most major US brokers moved to
**zero commissions** on US stocks and ETFs in 2019, so for typical retail equity trading the
explicit commission is often $0. Options, futures, and some other products still carry per-contract
fees.

Zero commission does not mean trading is free — see spread and payment for order flow below.

## The bid-ask spread is a real cost

Every trade crosses the spread between the bid and the ask. You buy at the ask and sell at the
bid, and the difference is a cost you pay even when commission is zero. On a liquid stock the
spread is a penny or two; on a thin one it can be far larger than any commission ever was.

This is why "commission-free" is not the same as "cost-free." The spread is the cost that did not
go away.

## Regulatory fees

Small regulatory fees apply to sales — the SEC fee and FINRA's Trading Activity Fee. They are
fractions of a cent per share and usually round to pennies on a retail-sized order, but they exist
and appear on trade confirmations.

## Why frequent trading is expensive

Each round trip pays the spread twice — once to enter, once to exit. A strategy that trades often
pays that cost on every trade, and those small costs compound into a large drag over time. This is
a major reason active trading underperforms buying and holding for most people.
