# Pattern Day Trader (PDT) Rule

## What counts as a day trade

A day trade is buying and selling the same security on the same trading day, in the same account.
Buying on Monday and selling on Tuesday is not a day trade. Buying and selling twice on Monday is
two day trades.

## When you are classified as a pattern day trader

You are flagged as a pattern day trader if you execute **four or more day trades within five
business days**, and those day trades represent **more than 6% of your total trading activity**
in that same period.

Both conditions must hold. Four day trades alone do not trigger the classification if they are a
small fraction of an otherwise active account.

## The $25,000 minimum equity requirement

A flagged pattern day trader must maintain **at least $25,000 in account equity** on any day they
day trade. This equity must be in the account *before* the day trading begins — meeting it later
in the day does not retroactively permit the trade.

Equity may be a combination of cash and eligible securities.

## What happens if equity falls below the minimum

If a flagged account drops below $25,000, day trading is prohibited until the balance is restored.
The account is restricted to closing-only transactions until the deposit clears. Deposits are
subject to a settlement period, so restoring access is not instant.

## Why this rule exists

The rule exists to ensure that people taking on the amplified risk of frequent intraday leverage
have a capital cushion to absorb losses. It is a FINRA rule, not a broker preference, and it
cannot be waived by request.
