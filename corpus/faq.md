# Frequently Asked Questions

## What is a stock?

A stock is a share of ownership in a company. Owning one share makes you a part-owner, entitled to
a slice of the company's future profits and, sometimes, a vote in its decisions. Prices move as
buyers and sellers disagree about what those future profits are worth.

## What is an ETF?

An ETF (exchange-traded fund) is a single security that holds a basket of many stocks or other
assets. Buying one share of a broad-market ETF gives you a small piece of hundreds of companies at
once, which spreads risk without buying each stock individually. ETFs trade throughout the day like
a stock.

## What does it mean to go long?

Going long simply means buying a security expecting it to rise — the ordinary way most people
invest. It is the opposite of going short, which bets on a fall.

## Why did my order not fill?

The most common reason is a limit order whose price the market never reached. A buy limit below the
current price, or a sell limit above it, waits until the stock moves to your price — and if it
never does, the order expires unfilled. A market order, by contrast, fills almost immediately
because it accepts the current price.

## What is the difference between a stock's price and its value?

Price is what the market will pay for a share right now. Value is what the underlying business is
actually worth, which is a matter of judgement and disagreement. The two are related but not the
same, and the gap between them is where both opportunity and risk live.

## Can this assistant tell me what to buy?

No. It explains how trading works and can walk you through the mechanics and trade-offs, but it
does not recommend specific investments — that would be financial advice, which requires a license
and knowledge of your personal situation.
