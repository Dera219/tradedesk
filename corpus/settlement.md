# Settlement and T+1

## What settlement is

Settlement is the moment ownership and cash actually change hands, which happens *after* the trade
executes. When you buy a stock, the execution is instant but the transfer of shares for money
completes on the settlement date.

## The T+1 settlement cycle

US stock trades settle on **T+1**: trade date plus one business day. A stock bought on Monday
settles Tuesday. This became the standard in May 2024, shortened from the older T+2 cycle.
Weekends and market holidays do not count as business days.

## Why settlement matters for a cash account

In a cash account, you must pay for a purchase with settled funds. Proceeds from a sale are not
settled until T+1, so using them to buy again before they settle, then selling the new position
before *those* funds settle, can trigger a **good-faith violation**. Repeated violations get the
account restricted to settled-cash-only trading for 90 days.

Margin accounts avoid this because the broker fronts the unsettled funds — which is a different
set of risks, not a free pass.
