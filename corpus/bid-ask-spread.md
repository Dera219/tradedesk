# Bid, Ask, and Spread

## The bid

The bid is the highest price a buyer in the market is currently willing to pay for a share. If you
want to sell right now with a market order, the bid is roughly what you receive.

## The ask

The ask (or offer) is the lowest price a seller is currently willing to accept. If you want to buy
right now with a market order, the ask is roughly what you pay. The ask is always higher than the
bid.

## The spread

The spread is the gap between the bid and the ask. It is the market maker's compensation for
standing ready to trade, and it is a cost every trade pays: you buy at the ask and sell at the
bid, losing the spread on the round trip even with zero commission.

## What the spread tells you about a stock

A **narrow** spread (a penny or two) signals a liquid, heavily traded stock — easy to enter and
exit near a single price. A **wide** spread signals thin trading, where buying and selling both
happen at meaningfully worse prices. Spread width is one of the clearest readouts of how liquid a
stock actually is.

## The last price is not the price you get

Quote screens show a "last" price — the price of the most recent trade. It is history. What you
will actually pay or receive is set by the current bid and ask, which can differ from the last
price, especially in a fast market or a thin stock.
