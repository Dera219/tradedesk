# Time in Force

## What time in force means

Time in force tells the broker how long an order stays active before it is automatically
cancelled. It matters for any order that might not fill immediately — a limit order sitting away
from the current price, for example.

## Day order

A day order is active only for the current trading session. If it has not filled by the market
close, it is cancelled automatically. This is the default for most orders. Placing a day order
after hours queues it for the next session's open, not the current moment.

## Good-till-cancelled (GTC)

A good-till-cancelled order stays active across multiple trading days until it fills or you cancel
it. Brokers usually cap how long a GTC order can live — commonly 30 to 90 days — after which it
expires on its own.

A GTC order is easy to forget. A limit you set weeks ago can fill on a day you were not watching,
because market conditions changed and the price finally reached it.
