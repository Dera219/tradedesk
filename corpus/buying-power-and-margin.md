# Buying Power and Margin

## What buying power is

Buying power is the total dollar amount of securities you can purchase right now. In a cash
account it equals your settled cash. In a margin account it is larger than your cash, because the
broker lends against your holdings.

## What margin is

Margin is borrowing money from the broker to buy securities, using the securities in your account
as collateral. It magnifies both gains and losses: with 2:1 margin, a 10% move in the stock is a
20% move in your equity, in whichever direction.

Buying on margin means paying interest on the borrowed amount for as long as you hold the
position. That interest is a cost that accrues daily whether the position is up or down.

## Maintenance margin and the margin call

After buying on margin, you must keep your equity above a **maintenance margin** — commonly 25% of
the position's market value, though brokers often set it higher. If the position falls enough that
your equity drops below this level, the broker issues a **margin call**.

A margin call demands you deposit cash or sell holdings to restore the minimum. If you do not act,
the broker can sell your positions without your permission, at whatever price the market offers,
to protect its loan. This can happen at the worst possible moment, locking in losses.

## Why margin amplifies risk

The danger of margin is not just larger losses — it is forced selling. An unleveraged holder can
wait out a downturn; a leveraged one can be sold out at the bottom by a margin call and never
participate in the recovery. Leverage removes your ability to simply hold.
